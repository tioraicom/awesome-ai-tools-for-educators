74 tools for the work of teaching, grouped by the part of the week they belong to: planning
a lesson, making the materials, differentiating a text, running the room, reading a class set,
and the admin around all of it.

One rule decides what is here. A tool may prepare a judgement about a student's work; it may
not make one. Every marking entry shows what produced its score, and the products sold to
schools as certainty about who cheated are absent on purpose.

Maintained by [TiorAI](https://tiorai.com/), which catalogues AI tools for a living.

## Lesson planning

Producing the plan, the worksheet, and the materials that go with a lesson.

- **[Almanack](https://www.almanack.ai/)** — Plans a lesson from a curriculum standard and keeps the standard visible in the output. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Brisk Teaching](https://www.briskteaching.com/)** — Works inside Google Docs and Classroom, so the plan is written where the class already lives. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Curipod](https://curipod.com/)** — Turns a topic into an interactive lesson with polls and writing prompts built in. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Eduaide](https://www.eduaide.ai/)** — A library of over a hundred resource types rather than one general prompt box. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[MagicSchool AI](https://www.magicschool.ai/)** — The broadest set of teacher-shaped generators, from rubrics to parent emails. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[TeacherMatic](https://teachermatic.com/)** — Built for further and higher education, with generators aimed at course-level work. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Teachy](https://teachy.ai/)** — Builds a lesson, its activities, and its assessment as one linked set. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Twee](https://twee.com/)** — Language teaching specifically: questions, dialogues, and gap fills from any text or video. `Freemium` `Web`

- **[Goblin Tools](https://goblin.tools/)** — Breaks a task into steps at the granularity a student asks for, which suits executive-function support. `Free` `Web` `iOS` `Android`
- **[Helperbird](https://www.helperbird.com/)** — Reading rulers, dyslexia fonts, and text-to-speech applied to any page a class is on. `Freemium` `Browser Extension`
- **[Microsoft Immersive Reader](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/education/learning-tools)** — Line focus, syllable breaks, and read-aloud already present in the Office apps a school runs. `Free` `Web` `Windows` `iOS` `Android`
- **[NaturalReader](https://www.naturalreaders.com/)** — Converts a worksheet or a scanned page into audio a student can take home. `Freemium` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `iOS` `Android` `Browser Extension`
- **[Read&Write](https://www.everway.com/products/read-and-write-education/)** — A support toolbar many schools already licence, covering prediction, dictionaries, and audio. `Freemium` `Web` `Windows` `macOS` `Browser Extension`
- **[Rewordify](https://www.rewordify.com/)** — Simplifies difficult English and shows the original word beside the replacement. `Free` `Web`

## Marking and feedback

Speeding up the reading of student work while the grade stays with the teacher.

- **[Class Companion](https://www.classcompanion.com/)** — Gives students feedback on a draft and reports to the teacher what the class got wrong. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[EssayGrader](https://essaygrader.ai/)** — Drafts comments on written work that a teacher then edits before returning. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Gradescope](https://www.gradescope.com/)** — Groups identical answers so one comment reaches every student who wrote it. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Mote](https://www.mote.com/)** — Voice comments on student work, transcribed, which say more in less marking time. `Freemium` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Quill](https://www.quill.org/)** — Free writing practice that returns targeted revision on one sentence at a time. `Free` `Web`
- **[Writable](https://www.writable.com/)** — Runs a draft-and-revise cycle where the feedback arrives between drafts rather than at the end. `Paid` `Web`

## How tools are selected

A tool qualifies when it does part of the **teaching job**: planning a lesson, making the
materials, differentiating a text, running an activity, reading a class set of work, or
handling the admin around all of it. It has to be usable by one teacher without a
procurement process, which in practice means a free tier, an individual plan, or an education
licence a school already holds.

### The rule that does most of the work here

**The tool prepares the judgement; the teacher makes it.** Every marking and feedback tool in
this list shows what produced a score — the rubric criterion, the sentence it objected to, the
working it followed — and leaves a person to submit the grade.

Anything that returns a verdict about a named student without showing its evidence is out, and
that exclusion is what removed most of the candidates in that category.

AI-content detectors are out entirely, and this is the one place the list refuses a product
teachers are actively sold. They are marketed as certainty and they do not deliver it; a false
positive is an accusation, and the person it lands on is a student who cannot audit it. A list
for teachers should not recommend a tool whose errors are paid for by somebody else.

### What the categories are telling you

The grouping follows the shape of a teacher's week rather than the shape of the software
market, and two distinctions inside it are worth knowing:

- **Explaining and tutoring** is the only group a student talks to directly. Every entry in it
  lets the teacher set the boundary and read the transcript afterwards.
- **Marking and feedback** never returns a final grade on its own. What it returns is a draft
  with its reasoning attached.

The rest is preparation, and preparation is where the time actually goes.

### What is excluded

AI-content detectors, all of them. Proctoring and exam-surveillance platforms. Student
information systems, attendance, and timetabling, which are institutional purchases rather
than teaching tools. Anything that grades a named student without showing what produced the
mark. Homework answer services, for the reason `awesome-ai-tools-for-students` gives at
length.

Personal productivity software is also out of scope. A teacher's inbox and calendar are the
same inbox and calendar as everybody else's, and `awesome-ai-productivity-tools` covers them
properly rather than in one thin category here.

Entries are ordered alphabetically inside each category. Position carries no meaning, and
there is no paid placement, sponsorship, or affiliate link anywhere in this repository.

## Disclaimer

Everything listed is built and operated by a third party, not by TiorAI. Pricing, plan
limits, and terms change without notice, and a tool that fits today may not next quarter.

**Your school's data rules come first.** Pasting a piece of student work into a third-party
tool is a data-processing decision, and whether it is permitted depends on your institution,
your jurisdiction, and the contract the school holds with that vendor. A tool being listed here
is not evidence that you may put a child's work into it. Check before you do, and prefer the
versions your school has already licensed.

Every tool here can be wrong, and the marking tools are wrong in the way that matters most:
confidently, and about a specific student. Read what they produce before it reaches a pupil or
a parent.

Listing here is editorial selection, not endorsement.
